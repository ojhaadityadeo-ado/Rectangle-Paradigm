Visual Proof: Fractal Geometric Series

Decomposing a unit square to visually prove that an infinite geometric series converges perfectly to 1.

$$ \text{Total Area} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3^{n-1}}{4^n} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{16} + \frac{9}{64} + \dots = 1 $$

Proof Progression

Step 0: Initial State We begin with an empty unit square. Total Area = $0$.
Step 1 ($n=1$) The unit square is divided into 4 quadrants, and the top-left one is colored cyan. Area = $\frac{1}{4}$.
Step 2 ($n=2$) In each of the three remaining empty squares, the process repeats. This adds three new, smaller squares. Added Area = $3 \times \frac{1}{16} = \frac{3}{16}$.
Step 3 ($n=3$) Now there are 9 remaining small empty squares. Splitting them adds nine even smaller squares. Added Area = $9 \times \frac{1}{64} = \frac{9}{64}$.
Step 4: Iterative Pattern This forms a deterministic fractal. At step $n$, we add $3^{n-1}$ squares, each of area $\frac{1}{4^n}$.
Step 5: Infinite Convergence As $n \to \infty$, the square is almost entirely filled with small cyan points, leading to a visual convergence where the area is fully consumed, totaling $1$.

Mathematical Derivation

1. Series Breakdown

We define the series we are visually constructing. The "From Visual" boxes trace each color-coded area from the graphic back to its corresponding term in the series:

$$ \text{Total Area} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3^{n-1}}{4^n} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{16} + \frac{9}{64} + \dots $$

Each term precisely matches the area added in each successive step of the animation.

2. Summation Calculation

We treat this sequence as an infinite geometric summation:

  • First Term ($a$): $\frac{1}{4}$ (The first colored area).
  • Common Ratio ($r$): $\frac{3}{4}$ (Each step adds three times as many squares that are one-fourth the previous size; meaning we are taking exactly three-fourths of the remaining area).

Using the standard geometric sum formula $S = \frac{a}{1-r}$:

$$ S = \frac{1/4}{1 - 3/4} = \frac{1/4}{1/4} = 1 $$
