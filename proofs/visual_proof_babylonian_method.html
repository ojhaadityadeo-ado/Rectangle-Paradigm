The Babylonian Method for $\sqrt{S}$

A beautiful geometric iteration: transforming a rectangle of area $S$ into a perfect square by successively averaging its side lengths.

$$ x_{n+1} = \frac{x_n + y_n}{2} \quad \text{where} \quad y_n = \frac{S}{x_n} $$

Iteration Progression

Iteration 0: The Initial Guess We want a square of area $S$. We start with a guess width $x_0$. To keep the area strictly equal to $S$, the height must be $y_0 = S / x_0$. The true root $\sqrt{S}$ lies between them.
Iteration 1: The First Average We average the sides to form a better approximation: $x_1 = (x_0 + y_0)/2$. The new height adjusts to $y_1 = S / x_1$ to conserve the exact area $S$.
Iteration 2: Halving the Error We repeat the process, taking $x_2 = (x_1 + y_1)/2$. Notice how rapidly the rectangle squishes towards a perfect square. This is "quadratic convergence".
Iteration 3: Near Perfection After just three averages, the width and height are nearly indistinguishable from one another. The gap between $x_n$ and $y_n$ closes exponentially fast.
Iteration 4: The Square Limit The rectangle has functionally transformed into a perfect square. The width and height are mathematically equal to $\sqrt{S}$ within the bounds of standard precision.

Mathematical Foundation: Heron's Method

1. The Geometric Algorithm

The goal is to find a number $x$ such that $x^2 = S$. If our current guess $x_n$ is too small ($x_n < \sqrt{S}$), then forcing an area of $S$ means the corresponding height $y_n = \frac{S}{x_n}$ will be strictly too large ($y_n > \sqrt{S}$).

Because the true root $\sqrt{S}$ is always trapped directly between $x_n$ and $y_n$, taking their arithmetic mean yields an extremely accurate new guess:

$$ x_{n+1} = \frac{x_n + y_n}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(x_n + \frac{S}{x_n}\right) $$

2. Quadratic Convergence

This method (also known as Heron's Method or Newton's Method applied to $f(x) = x^2 - S$) is famous for its quadratic convergence.

This means that the number of correct decimal places approximately doubles with each iteration! If we define the relative error $\epsilon_n = \frac{x_n - \sqrt{S}}{\sqrt{S}}$, the math shows:

$$ \epsilon_{n+1} = \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2(1 + \epsilon_n)} \approx \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_n^2 $$

Squaring a small error (like $0.01^2 = 0.0001$) guarantees the rapid morphing into a perfect square seen in the visualizer.
