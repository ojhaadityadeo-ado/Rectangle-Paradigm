Visual Proof: Cosine Double-Angle

Deriving $\cos(2\theta)$ using the dot product of symmetric unit vectors, and transforming it visually using a Unit Area bar.

$$ \cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta $$

Proof Progression

1. The Unit Vectors Construct two unit vectors symmetric across the x-axis: $\mathbf{u}$ at angle $\theta$, and $\mathbf{v}$ at angle $-\theta$.
2. Dot Product (Geometry) The total angle between $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is $2\theta$. By definition, the dot product of two unit vectors is the cosine of the angle between them: $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \cos(2\theta)$.
3. Dot Product (Algebra) Multiplying their coordinate components yields the exact same dot product mathematically: $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = (\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) + (\sin\theta)(-\sin\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$.
4. Area Representation We can represent this difference visually. A "Unit Bar" (Total Area = $1$) is naturally split into $\cos^2\theta$ and $\sin^2\theta$ via the Pythagorean Identity.
5. Visual Subtraction To calculate $\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$, we slide a red subtraction block up. The remaining unshaded top area is exactly our target: $\cos(2\theta)$.
6. Alternative Forms Looking at the entire unit bar, the remaining $\cos(2\theta)$ section is precisely the whole $1$ minus two red blocks. Thus, $\cos(2\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$.

Mathematical Analysis

1. Equating the Dot Product

The elegance of this proof lies in evaluating the dot product $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}$ in two fundamentally different ways and setting them equal.

  • Geometrically: $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|\cos(\text{angle}) = (1)(1)\cos(2\theta)$.
  • Algebraically: $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = x_u x_v + y_u y_v = (\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) + (\sin\theta)(-\sin\theta)$.

This avoids entirely the messy triangle constructions typically required for angle addition proofs, arriving cleanly at $\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$.

2. The Unit Bar Transformations

The fundamental Pythagorean identity states that $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$. This means any $1 \times 1$ physical area can be partitioned perfectly into these two components.

We can substitute this identity into our primary formula to derive the two alternate forms:

$\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$
$\text{Sub } \cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta:$
$\implies \cos(2\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$
--- OR ---
$\text{Sub } \sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta:$
$\implies \cos(2\theta) = 2\cos^2\theta - 1$
