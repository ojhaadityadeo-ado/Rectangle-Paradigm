Geometric Breakdown

1. The Outer Square

The side length is $p + q$. Since we are given $p + q = 1$, the total area of the bounding box is exactly $1$.

2. The Four Inner Rectangles

We place 4 shaded rectangles in a pinwheel. Each rectangle has dimensions $p \times q$.

Area = 4pq 4(0.75)(0.25) = 0.750

3. The Geometric Inequality

The 4 rectangles fit entirely inside the unit square, leaving an empty central gap. Therefore, their area must be $\le 1$.

$4pq \le 1$ 0.750 $\le 1$

Algebraic Substitution

The visual proves undeniably that $4pq \le 1$. We can transform this simple observation into our target inequality using basic algebra.

Since $p$ and $q$ are positive fractions, their product $pq$ is positive. We can divide both sides of our inequality by $pq$:

Transforming the Expression

$$ 4pq \le 1 \implies 4 \le \frac{1}{pq} $$

Because we were given the constraint that $p + q = 1$, we can ingeniously substitute $1$ with $(p+q)$ in the numerator!

$$ 4 \le \frac{p+q}{pq} $$

Splitting the fraction gives us the final elegant result:

$$ 4 \le \frac{p}{pq} + \frac{q}{pq} \implies 4 \le \frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{p} $$

Equality ($1/p + 1/q = 4$) occurs strictly when the central gap vanishes. As seen in the interactive visual, the gap area $(p-q)^2$ only vanishes when $p = q = 0.5$.

$$ \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} \ge 4 $$
