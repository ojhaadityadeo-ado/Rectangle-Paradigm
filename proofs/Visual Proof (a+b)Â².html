How to understand this proof:

Imagine a large square. We want the length of each side of this square to be $(a + b)$.
Because the area of any square is its side length multiplied by itself, the total area of this big square is exactly $(a + b)^2$.

Now, look at the visualization above. We can split this large square into four smaller distinct pieces:

  • One blue square: Its sides are $a$ and $a$, so its area is $a^2$.
  • One orange square: Its sides are $b$ and $b$, so its area is $b^2$.
  • Two green rectangles: Each has sides $a$ and $b$, so each has an area of $ab$. (Together, they make $2ab$).

Because these four smaller pieces fit perfectly inside the large square without any gaps or overlaps, their total combined area must equal the area of the large square. Therefore:

$$(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$$
