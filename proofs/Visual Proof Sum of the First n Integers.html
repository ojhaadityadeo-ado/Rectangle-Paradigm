$$S = 1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n$$

The Staircase

Consider a staircase built from $1, 2, 3, \dots, n$ unit blocks. Its total area represents the exact sum we want to calculate.

Sum = 21

The Geometric Insight

To find the sum of the first $n$ positive integers ($1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n$), we can represent each number as a stack of square blocks. Stacking them side-by-side creates a geometric "staircase" shape.

Instead of counting the blocks one by one, we use a clever spatial trick: we take a second, identical copy of the staircase.

Interlocking to form a Rectangle

By turning the second staircase upside down ($180^\circ$ rotation), the "steps" of the two shapes match perfectly. Sliding them together forms a solid, gapless rectangle.

  • The width of this rectangle is simply the number of columns: $n$.
  • The height combines the tallest column ($n$) with the shortest ($1$), making the total height $n + 1$.

The Final Formula

The area of the entire rectangle is $n \times (n+1)$. Since this rectangle is made of exactly two identical staircases, the area of our original single staircase (the sum) is exactly half of that.

$$ \sum_{i=1}^{n} i = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} $$
