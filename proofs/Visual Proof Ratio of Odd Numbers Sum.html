The Geometric Insight: Gnomons

To visually prove that the ratio of the sum of the first $n$ odd numbers to the next $n$ odd numbers is $\frac{1}{3}$, we use the classic L-shape (gnomon) area of a square concept.

Every odd number visually represents an "L-shaped" border added around a square. Therefore, the sum of the first $k$ odd numbers perfectly forms a solid $k \times k$ square with an area of $k^2$.

Comparing Quadrants

  • First $n$ odds: Forms an $n \times n$ square. This takes up exactly $1$ quadrant of a larger $2n \times 2n$ grid.
  • Total $2n$ odds: Forms a $2n \times 2n$ square. This takes up all $4$ quadrants.
  • Next $n$ odds: Since they are the outer borders added to the first $n$ odds, they perfectly fill the remaining $3$ quadrants.

Therefore, $\frac{\text{First } n}{\text{Next } n} = \frac{1 \text{ quadrant}}{3 \text{ quadrants}} = \frac{1}{3}$.

$$ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (2i-1)}{\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n} (2i-1)} = \frac{n^2}{3n^2} = \frac{1}{3} $$
