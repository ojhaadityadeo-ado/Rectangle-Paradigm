Proof Progression

1. The Sample Space ($\Omega$) We start with a $1 \times 1$ unit square. Total area equals $1$.
2. Partition the Space ($A_i$) Slice the square vertically into mutually exclusive and exhaustive events $A_1, A_2, A_3$. Their widths sum to $1$.
3. Intersection $B \cap A_1$ Event $B$ occupies a fraction of $A_1$ equal to its conditional probability $P(B|A_1)$. Area is $P(A_1) \cdot P(B|A_1)$.
4. Evaluate all Intersections Repeat this process for $A_2$ and $A_3$, identifying the area of $B$ in each vertical strip.
5. The Total Area of $B$ The total area of $B$ across the entire sample space is simply the geometric sum of all these sub-rectangular slices.

Deriving the Algebraic Property

1. Partitioning the Space

The vertical strips represent a set of mutually exclusive and exhaustive events $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$. Because they cover the entire sample space without overlapping, their widths sum perfectly to $1$:

$$ \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A_i) = 1 $$

This forms the base width for our entire calculation.

2. Summing the Rectangular Slices

To find the total probability of event $B$, we look at its area within each strip. The multiplication rule defines the area of the rectangle in strip $i$ as:

$$ \text{Area}_i = \text{Width}_i \times \text{Height}_i = P(A_i) \cdot P(B \mid A_i) $$

Because event $B$ only exists within these discrete non-overlapping strips, its total area is the sum of these rectangular parts.

Conclusion: The Law of Total Probability

By calculating the area of event $B$ piece by piece across the partition, we visually arrive at the standard formula for the Law of Total Probability:

$$ P(B) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(A_i) P(B \mid A_i) $$

This geometric representation makes it immediately obvious why we multiply the conditional probability (the vertical height) by the partition probability (the horizontal width) and add them together.
