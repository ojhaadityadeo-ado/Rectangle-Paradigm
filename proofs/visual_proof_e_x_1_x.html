Proof Progression

1. The Baseline: $e^t \ge 1$ Since $e^0 = 1$ and $e^t$ is strictly increasing, for all $t \ge 0$, the exponential curve stays strictly on or above the horizontal line $y = 1$.
2. Area Under the Curve Integrating $y = e^t$ from $t=0$ to $t=x$ yields the entire shaded blue area: $\int_0^x e^t \, dt = [e^t]_0^x = e^x - 1$.
3. The Inscribed Rectangle Within the exact same interval $[0, x]$, the line $y = 1$ bounds a solid green rectangle of base $x$ and height $1$. Its area is exactly $x \times 1 = x$.
4. Comparing the Areas Because $e^t \ge 1$, the integral region completely contains the $x \times 1$ rectangle. The golden hatched remainder represents non-negative excess area.
5. The Conclusion Therefore, $\text{Total Area} \ge \text{Rectangle Area} \implies e^x - 1 \ge x$. Adding $1$ to both sides gives $e^x \ge 1 + x$.

Mathematical Analysis & Extensions

1. The Definite Integral Argument

The fundamental property of integration preserves inequalities: if $f(t) \ge g(t)$ on $[0, x]$, then $\int_0^x f(t)\,dt \ge \int_0^x g(t)\,dt$.

$$ \int_0^x e^t \, dt \ge \int_0^x 1 \, dt $$

Evaluating the anti-derivatives directly:

$$ \big[e^t\big]_0^x \ge \big[t\big]_0^x \implies e^x - 1 \ge x \implies e^x \ge 1 + x $$

2. The Tangent Line Interpretation

Why does $1+x$ appear? Consider the tangent line to $g(x) = e^x$ at $x = 0$:

  • The value at origin is $g(0) = e^0 = 1$.
  • The slope at origin is $g'(0) = e^0 = 1$.
  • The equation of the tangent line is $y = g(0) + g'(0)(x - 0) = 1 + x$.

Because the second derivative $g''(x) = e^x > 0$ for all real $x$, the exponential curve is strictly convex. A convex function always lies on or above every one of its tangent lines!

What About Negative Values ($x < 0$)?

The convexity proof holds universally for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. If proving via integrals for negative $x$, note that for $t < 0$, we have $e^t \le 1$. Integrating from $x$ to $0$:

$$ \int_x^0 e^t \, dt \le \int_x^0 1 \, dt \implies 1 - e^x \le -x \implies e^x \ge 1 + x $$

Thus, equality $e^x = 1 + x$ holds strictly if and only if $x = 0$. For any other real number, $e^x > 1 + x$.
