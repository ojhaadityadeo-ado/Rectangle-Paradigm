Visual Proof: Algebraic Inequality

A geometric demonstration of why the difference of squares is strictly greater than the square of their difference.

$$a^2 - b^2 > (a-b)^2 \quad \text{for } a > b > 0$$
Side a 8.0
Side b 3.0

Proof Progression

1. The Original Square We start with a large square of side length $a$. Its total enclosed area is exactly $a^2$.
2. The Left Side: $a^2 - b^2$ Subtract a smaller square of side $b$ from the top-right corner. The remaining blue L-shaped region represents exactly $a^2 - b^2$.
3. The Right Side: $(a-b)^2$ Look at the bottom-left corner of the L-shape. It forms a perfect square with side length $(a-b)$. Its area is exactly $(a-b)^2$.
4. The Gained Area Notice that the L-shape contains $(a-b)^2$ plus two extra rectangular regions. Each rectangle has an area of $b(a-b)$.
5. Conclusion Because $a > b$, these two extra rectangles have positive area. Therefore, the L-shape ($a^2 - b^2$) is strictly greater than the smaller square $(a-b)^2$.

Deriving the Inequality Algebraically

1. Algebraic Expansion

We can also prove this strictly using algebra. Let's expand the right side of the inequality using the standard binomial formula:

$$ (a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2 $$

Now, we want to compare this expanded form to our left side, which is $a^2 - b^2$.

2. Analyzing the Difference

Let's subtract the Right Side from the Left Side to see if the difference is strictly positive:

$$ (a^2 - b^2) - (a^2 - 2ab + b^2) $$ $$ = a^2 - b^2 - a^2 + 2ab - b^2 $$ $$ = 2ab - 2b^2 = 2b(a-b) $$

Conclusion

The difference between the two sides simplifies exactly to $2b(a-b)$. This perfectly matches the two extra rectangles we found in our visual proof!

$$ a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)^2 + 2b(a-b) $$

Since we stated that $a > b > 0$, both $b$ and $(a-b)$ are positive numbers. Therefore, the extra area $2b(a-b) > 0$. Because we have to add a positive amount to $(a-b)^2$ to reach $a^2 - b^2$, we conclusively prove that $a^2 - b^2$ is strictly greater.
