Visual Proof: Telescoping Series

Slicing a unit square to demonstrate how an infinite sum of fractions perfectly converges to exactly 1.

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+1)} = 1 $$

Proof Progression

1. The Unit Square We start with a square of total area $1$. The very first term $(n=1)$ is $\frac{1}{2}$, which covers exactly half the square.
2. Splitting the Terms The next term is $\frac{1}{6}$. Notice that $\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3}$. It perfectly slots into the empty space.
3. Telescoping Sum As we add more fractions, adjacent terms cancel each other out: $\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right) + \dots$
4. The Empty Gap After $N$ terms, the shaded area spans exactly $1 - \frac{1}{N+1}$. The remaining empty gap is precisely $\frac{1}{N+1}$.
5. The Infinite Limit As $N \to \infty$, the gap $\frac{1}{N+1}$ shrinks to absolute zero. The infinite series perfectly fills the entire unit area!

Deriving the Algebraic Identity

1. Partial Fraction Decomposition

The key to this proof lies in recognizing that every single term in our infinite series can be split into the difference of two simpler fractions.

$$ \frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} $$

Geometrically, this corresponds to the width of the $n$-th rectangular strip in our diagram. It begins at the $x$-coordinate $\frac{n-1}{n}$ and ends exactly at $\frac{n}{n+1}$.

2. The Telescoping Collapse

When we sum the first $N$ terms together, a beautiful pattern emerges. Each negative fraction perfectly cancels out the positive fraction from the subsequent term.

$$ S_N = \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\right) + \dots + \left(\frac{1}{N} - \frac{1}{N+1}\right) $$

Everything in the middle vanishes. The only terms that survive the collapse are the very first number and the very last fraction:

$$ S_N = 1 - \frac{1}{N+1} $$

Conclusion: The Infinite Limit

We've established that the sum of the first $N$ terms leaves exactly a $\frac{1}{N+1}$ gap of unfilled area in our unit square. To find the sum of the entire infinite series, we take the limit as $N$ approaches infinity:

$$ \lim_{N \to \infty} S_N = \lim_{N \to \infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{N+1} \right) = 1 $$

Because the term $\frac{1}{N+1}$ shrinks to absolute zero, the entire area of the $1 \times 1$ square is completely filled by the infinite sequence of strips.
