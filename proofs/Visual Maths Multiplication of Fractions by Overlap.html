Multiplication of Fractions by Shading Overlap

Shade one fraction vertically and another fraction horizontally. The part that gets both shadings is the overlap, and that overlap shows the product.

\(\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\)

What the picture means

The overlap is the answer

We split the whole rectangle into equal small parts. One fraction chooses some columns. The other fraction chooses some rows. The little parts that lie in both choices give the product.

Multiplication sentence
\(\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\)
What is happening?
The whole rectangle is split into \(3\) columns and \(4\) rows, so there are \(3\times4=12\) little parts. We shade \(2\) of the \(3\) columns and \(3\) of the \(4\) rows. The overlap has \(2\times3=6\) little parts, so the product is \(\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\).

How to explain this to small kids

Start with one whole rectangle. This whole rectangle stands for \(1\).
Cut it into columns and rows. If the first fraction is \(\frac{a}{b}\), make \(b\) equal columns. If the second fraction is \(\frac{c}{d}\), make \(d\) equal rows.
Shade the first fraction. Shade \(a\) of the \(b\) columns.
Shade the second fraction. Shade \(c\) of the \(d\) rows.
Look at the overlap. The overlap has \(a\times c\) little pieces out of \(b\times d\) little pieces altogether. So \[ \frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ac}{bd}. \]
\(\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\)
