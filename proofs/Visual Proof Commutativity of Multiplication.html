Deriving the Commutative Property

1. The Geometric Definition of Area

In Euclidean geometry, the area of a rectangle is intrinsically defined by its two orthogonal bounds: its horizontal width and its vertical height.

$$\text{Area} = \text{Width} \times \text{Height}$$

By assigning the variables $a$ to the width and $b$ to the height, the algebraic expression $a \cdot b$ perfectly models the surface area of our initial 2D shape.

2. Rigid Transformations

A fundamental axiom of geometry is that rigid transformations (translations, reflections, and rotations) are isometries—they completely preserve the distances and angles between all points.

Therefore, applying a $90^\circ$ rotation to the rectangle alters its orientation relative to our coordinate axes, but the total surface area remains strictly constant.

$$\text{Area}_{\text{Original}} = \text{Area}_{\text{Rotated}}$$

Conclusion: Equating the Expressions

Because the shape was rotated by exactly $90^\circ$, the side that formerly acted as the width ($a$) now acts as the vertical height. Conversely, the side that was the height ($b$) now rests horizontally as the new width.

By recalculating the area of the transformed shape according to our standard definition ($\text{Width} \times \text{Height}$), we arrive at the new algebraic expression $b \cdot a$. Because the physical area was preserved during the rotation, the math dictates absolute equality:

$$a \cdot b = b \cdot a$$
