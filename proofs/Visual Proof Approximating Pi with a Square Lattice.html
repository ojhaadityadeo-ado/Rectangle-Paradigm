Approximating $\pi$ using a Square Lattice

This proof was given by Carl Friedrich Gauss.

\(\lim_{r\to\infty} \frac{f(r)}{r^2} = \pi\)

Count the lattice points inside the circle, place one unit square around each counted point, and compare that total square area with the true area \(\pi r^2\). The only disagreement occurs near the boundary, and that boundary error becomes small relative to \(r^2\) as the circle grows.

1. The Circle and the Lattice

Imagine the plane covered by a grid of unit squares. The corners are lattice points. We draw a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin.

Let $f(r)$ be the exact number of lattice points that lie inside or exactly on the circle.

2. Points into Squares

Around every counted lattice point, construct its corresponding $1 \times 1$ square.

Since each square has an area of $1$, the total area of this jagged blue region is exactly $f(r)$. Notice how it roughly approximates the area of the circle (\(\pi r^2\)).

3. The Boundary Error

The square region isn't a perfect circle. The area difference \(|f(r) - \pi r^2|\) comes entirely from the squares that intersect the circle's boundary.

These boundary squares (highlighted in orange) account for all the overshoots and undershoots in our approximation.

4. Bounding the Error

The maximum distance between any two points in a unit square is its diagonal, $\sqrt{2}$.

Therefore, every boundary square must fit entirely inside a thin red annulus with an inner radius of $r - \sqrt{2}$ and an outer radius of $r + \sqrt{2}$.

5. The Infinite Limit

The annulus gives a convenient upper bound for the boundary error. Its area is:
\( B(r) = \pi(r+\sqrt{2})^2 - \pi(r-\sqrt{2})^2 = 4\sqrt{2}\pi r \)

Since our boundary error is trapped inside this annulus, we get:
\( |f(r) - \pi r^2| < 4\sqrt{2}\pi r \)

Dividing by \(r^2\), the relative error shrinks to zero as \(r\) grows:

$$\left| \frac{f(r)}{r^2} - \pi \right| < \frac{4\sqrt{2}\pi}{r} \longrightarrow 0$$
