Visual Proof: Inequality $x + \frac{1}{x} \ge 2$

$$\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = 4 + \left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 \ge 4$$
Value of x
2.00

Area Breakdown

1. Four Rectangles

Each rectangle has sides $x$ and $1/x$. Their individual area is exactly $1$.

4 × (x · 1/x) = 4.00

2. Central Gap

The empty space forms a perfect square with side length $|x - 1/x|$.

(x - 1/x)² = 2.25

Total Area of Large Square:

Rectangles + Gap 4.00 + 2.25 = 6.25

The Geometric Insight

For any positive number $x$, we can build a square whose side length is exactly $x + \frac{1}{x}$. By partitioning this large square into a pinwheel pattern, we can geometrically prove that $x + \frac{1}{x} \ge 2$.

The total area of the large square is simply its side length squared: $\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2$.

Measuring the Inner Pieces

The area is also the sum of the four identical rectangles (each with area $x \cdot \frac{1}{x} = 1$) plus the area of the central square hole:

$$ \text{Total Area} = 4 + \left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 $$

Since the square of any real number can never be negative, the gap area must be $\ge 0$. This guarantees that the total area is strictly bounded $\ge 4$.

Because we assumed $x$ is positive, $x + \frac{1}{x}$ is also positive. We can safely take the square root of both sides of the inequality $\left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 \ge 4$ without flipping the sign:

$$ x + \frac{1}{x} \ge 2 $$
