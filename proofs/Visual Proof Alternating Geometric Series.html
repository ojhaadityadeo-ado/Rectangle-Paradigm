How to understand this proof:

  1. Start with a unit square: We assign the total area of the bounding square a value of $1$.
  2. Additions (Emerald Green): Adding a positive term to the series corresponds to shading a region of the square green.
  3. Subtractions (White with Red Border): Subtracting a term corresponds to cutting out/removing a region from the currently shaded area, returning it to white.
  4. The Alternating Pattern: We start by shading the whole square ($+1$). Then we divide it into a $3 \times 3$ grid and remove the bottom row ($-\frac{1}{3}$). Within that empty row, we add back two small corner triangles ($+\frac{1}{9}$). We then zoom into the central square and repeat this process infinitely.
  5. The Geometric Limit: As the pattern shrinks toward the center, the jagged edges converge into smooth diagonal lines that split the main square into four identical triangular quadrants.
  6. Conclusion: Only the bottom quadrant remains completely unshaded (empty). Since the four quadrants are identical and fill the area of $1$, the unshaded area is exactly $\frac{1}{4}$. Thus, the shaded area converging around it must be $1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$.
