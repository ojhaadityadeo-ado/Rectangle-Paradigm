The Geometric Insight

Start with a unit square of area $1$. Shade a fraction $(1-p)$ of it, then shade a fraction $(1-p)$ of what's left, and repeat this process recursively.

After $n+1$ cuts, the shaded region is built from alternating strips of area $(1-p),\ (1-p)p,\ (1-p)p^2,\ \ldots,\ (1-p)p^n$. This leaves a single unshaded corner with an area of exactly $p^{n+1}$.

Telescoping Sum

Since the shaded area and the unshaded corner must perfectly add up to the total area of the unit square ($1$), we establish the identity:

$$ (1-p)(1+p+p^2+\cdots+p^n) + p^{n+1} = 1 $$

Rearranging this gives us the sum of the finite geometric series:

$$ 1+p+p^2+\cdots+p^n = \frac{1-p^{n+1}}{1-p} $$

The Infinite Limit

As more cuts are made ($n \to \infty$), the unshaded corner $p^{n+1}$ shrinks toward zero because $0 < p < 1$. The shaded area then fills the entire square, proving the infinite series convergence:

$$ \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} p^i = \frac{1}{1-p} $$
