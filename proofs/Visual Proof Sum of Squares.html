The Geometric Insight

To prove that $3 \sum_{i=1}^n i^2 = (2n+1)\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$, we construct a visual proof showing that three sets of squares from $1^2$ up to $n^2$ can perfectly tile a specific rectangle.

We define the target rectangle to have a width of $(2n+1)$ and a height equal to the sum of the first $n$ integers: $(1 + 2 + \dots + n)$.
Note: The sum of the first $n$ integers is also known as the $n$-th triangular number.

The Tiling Strategy

For every color $i$ (where $i$ ranges from $1$ to $n$), the animation places exactly three $i \times i$ blocks into the rectangle:

  • One $i \times i$ block on the far left side.
  • One $i \times i$ block on the far right side.
  • A stepped tower strip in the middle. The tower strip is composed of $i$ rows, and their lengths are the first $i$ odd numbers ($1, 3, 5, \dots$). The sum of the first $i$ odd numbers is exactly $i^2$.

Since every color perfectly accounts for exactly $3 \times i^2$ blocks, and together all colors perfectly tile the $(2n+1) \times (1+2+\dots+n)$ rectangle with no gaps, the areas must be exactly equal:

$$ 3\sum_{i=1}^n i^2 = (2n+1)\sum_{i=1}^n i = (2n+1)\frac{n(n+1)}{2} $$
