Visual Proof: Area of a Rhombus

Bounding a rhombus inside a rectangle to geometrically prove that its area is exactly half the product of its diagonals.

$$ \text{Area}_{\text{rhombus}} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2 $$

Proof Progression

1. The Rhombus A rhombus is defined by two diagonals $d_1$ and $d_2$ that bisect each other at a perfect right angle.
2. The Bounding Rectangle Draw a bounding box tightly around the rhombus. This box is exactly a rectangle with width $d_1$ and height $d_2$. Its area is $d_1 \times d_2$.
3. Four Quadrants The extended diagonals divide the rectangle into four smaller, identical sub-rectangles.
4. Bisected Sub-rectangles Notice how the outer edges of the rhombus perfectly cut diagonally across each of the 4 sub-rectangles. This divides every sub-rectangle into two identical triangles (an inner and an outer).
5. The Geometric Fold If we fold the 4 outer corner triangles inward, they perfectly cover the rhombus! This proves the "outside" area is exactly equal to the "inside" area.
6. Conclusion Since the inner and outer areas are equal, the rhombus must take up exactly half of the total bounding rectangle.

Deriving the Area Formula

1. Rectangle Area

The bounding rectangle is constructed with its width parallel to the horizontal diagonal ($d_1$) and its height parallel to the vertical diagonal ($d_2$).

$$ \text{Area}_{\text{rect}} = \text{width} \times \text{height} = d_1 d_2 $$

2. Rhombus as Half of Rectangle

Because the edges of the rhombus act as diagonals to the 4 congruent sub-rectangles, the area inside the rhombus is identical to the area outside it. Thus, the rhombus fills exactly half the bounding box:

$$ \text{Area}_{\text{rhombus}} = \frac{1}{2} \text{Area}_{\text{rect}} $$

Conclusion

Substituting the formula for the rectangle's area directly gives us the standard formula for the area of any rhombus (and by extension, any kite, of which a rhombus is a symmetric special case):

$$ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2 $$
