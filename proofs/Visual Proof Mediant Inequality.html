Visual Proof: The Mediant Inequality

A beautiful geometric demonstration of why the mediant always lies strictly between its parent fractions.

$$\frac{a}{b} < \frac{a+c}{b+d} < \frac{c}{d}$$
Left Bound: a/b Right Bound: c/d Assumption: a/b < c/d
Step: 0 / 5

Proof Progression

1. Bounds (Left & Right) Areas of $a/b$ and $c/d$ on base $1$.
2. Split the Base Split base proportionally: $\frac{b}{b+d}$ and $\frac{d}{b+d}$.
3. The Mediant Area Build heights $a/b$ and $c/d$. Sum forms the Mediant.
4. Lower Bound Comparison Mediant gains area over the Left bound.
5. Upper Bound Comparison Mediant loses area under the Right bound.
6. Conclusion Left Area < Mediant Area < Right Area.

Notice the Left bound area ($a/b$) and Right bound area ($c/d$). Press Play to begin the visual proof.

The Geometric Proof Explained

1. The Left Shape
(The Lower Bound)

Dimensions: A single rectangle with a base width of $1$ and a uniform height of $\frac{a}{b}$.

Area:

$$\text{Area} = 1 \times \frac{a}{b} = \frac{a}{b}$$

2. The Middle Shape
(The Mediant)

The base width of $1$ is split into segments proportional to the denominators: $\frac{b}{b+d}$ and $\frac{d}{b+d}$.

  • Left Column: width $\frac{b}{b+d}$, height $\frac{a}{b}$.
    $\text{Area}_{\text{L}} = \frac{b}{b+d} \times \frac{a}{b} = \frac{a}{b+d}$
  • Right Column: width $\frac{d}{b+d}$, height $\frac{c}{d}$.
    $\text{Area}_{\text{R}} = \frac{d}{b+d} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c}{b+d}$

Total Area:

$$\frac{a}{b+d} + \frac{c}{b+d} = \frac{a+c}{b+d}$$

3. The Right Shape
(The Upper Bound)

Dimensions: A single rectangle with a base width of $1$ and a uniform height of $\frac{c}{d}$.

Area:

$$\text{Area} = 1 \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c}{d}$$

The Geometric Comparison

The core mathematical assumption is that $\frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d}$, meaning the height of the rightmost block is strictly taller than the height of the leftmost block.

Left vs. Middle: The middle shape matches the left shape's height ($\frac{a}{b}$) on its left side, but its right side steps up to the taller height ($\frac{c}{d}$). The pulsing green area in the animation explicitly shows this extra physical space gained by the middle shape over a uniform height of $\frac{a}{b}$. Therefore, the area of the middle shape is strictly larger than the left shape:
$$\frac{a}{b} < \frac{a+c}{b+d}$$
Middle vs. Right: The middle shape matches the right shape's height ($\frac{c}{d}$) on its right side, but its left side steps down to the shorter height ($\frac{a}{b}$). The pulsing red area shows the area that the middle shape is missing compared to a full block of height $\frac{c}{d}$. Therefore, the area of the middle shape is strictly smaller than the right shape:
$$\frac{a+c}{b+d} < \frac{c}{d}$$
