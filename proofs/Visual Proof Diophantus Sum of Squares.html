Visual Proof: Diophantus' Identity

A beautiful visual proof that factors two sums of squares into a new sum of two squares.

$$ (a^2 + b^2)(c^2 + d^2) = (ad + bc)^2 + (bd - ac)^2 $$

Proof Progression

1. The Initial Area Construct a large rectangle with width $(a^2 + b^2)$ and height $(c^2 + d^2)$. It contains four regions: $a^2c^2, b^2c^2, a^2d^2, b^2d^2$.
2. Rearranging into Squares The four sub-regions are geometrically reinterpreted as squares with side lengths $ac, bc, ad, bd$. Total area remains perfectly equal.
3. Geometric Decomposition We decompose $(ac)^2$ and $(bd)^2$ into one grey square $(bd - ac)^2$ and two identical rectangles of area $abcd$.
4. Reshaping the Rectangles The two $abcd$ rectangles are reshaped from $ac \times bd$ to $ad \times bc$. The mathematical area remains strictly unchanged.
5. Assembling the Identity Assemble the squares $(ad)^2, (bc)^2$ and the two reshaped rectangles to form a square of area $(ad + bc)^2$.
6. The Final Equation Added to the leftover grey square $(bd - ac)^2$, we have visually constructed the right side of Diophantus' Identity!

Deriving the Algebraic Identity

1. Expanding the Left Side

The proof begins by evaluating the initial bounding box, which corresponds to the left-hand side of our identity. By standard algebraic expansion (FOIL):

$$ (a^2 + b^2)(c^2 + d^2) = a^2c^2 + a^2d^2 + b^2c^2 + b^2d^2 $$

This directly represents the four initial regions seen in Step 1 of the visual proof.

2. Reconstructing the Right Side

During the animation, we isolate the terms $(ac)^2$ and $(bd)^2$. We mathematically rewrite their sum by introducing and subtracting the cross term $2abcd$:

$$ a^2c^2 + b^2d^2 = (bd - ac)^2 + 2abcd $$

By reshaping $2abcd$ to $2(ad)(bc)$ and adding it to the remaining $(ad)^2 + (bc)^2$, we form the perfect square $(ad+bc)^2$.
