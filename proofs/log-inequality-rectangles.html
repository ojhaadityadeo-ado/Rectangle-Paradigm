Why the bounds work

From t = 0 to t = x, the height 1/(1 + t) decreases from 1 to 1/(1 + x). Both rectangles have the same width, x.

Smaller rectangle ≤ shaded area ≤ larger rectangle. Both inequalities are strict for x > 0. At x = 0, all three areas are zero.
Why is the shaded area log(1 + x)?

The natural logarithm log(1 + x) is the area under 1/u from u = 1 to u = 1 + x. Relabeling the horizontal coordinate as u = 1 + t gives this same area under 1/(1 + t), from t = 0 to t = x.

What happens when −1 < x < 0?

Use the interval from x to 0, whose width is −x. The curve has height between 1 and 1/(1 + x), so its positive area A satisfies −x ≤ A ≤ −x/(1 + x).

But log(1 + x) = −A. Negating the area bounds reverses their order and gives the same inequality: x/(1 + x) ≤ log(1 + x) ≤ x. The restriction x > −1 is necessary for this real logarithm to be defined.

No area estimates are needed: the curve is contained between the two rectangles.
