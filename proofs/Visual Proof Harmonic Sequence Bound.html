Proof Progression

1. The Harmonic Blocks We construct $n$ rectangular blocks with width $1$ and decreasing heights $1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \dots, \frac{1}{n}$. Their total combined area is the harmonic number $H_n$.
2. Left Alignment: The Overestimate Place block $k$ on the interval $[k, k+1]$. Because $1/x$ is decreasing, the left edge touches the curve and the entire block sits above $1/x$. Thus, $H_n > \int_1^{n+1} \frac{1}{x}dx = \ln(n+1)$.
3. The Staircase Slide Drag the slide slider to shift the staircase left by 1 unit. The blocks now meet the curve at their right endpoints.
4. Right Alignment: The Underestimate Isolate the first block of area $1$. The remaining blocks on $[1, n]$ sit strictly below $1/x$. Their area is $H_n - 1 < \int_1^n \frac{1}{x}dx = \ln n \implies H_n < 1 + \ln n$.
5. The Harmonic Sandwich Combining both orientations yields the famous double inequality: $\ln(n+1) < H_n < 1 + \ln n$. The harmonic series grows logarithmically!

Deriving the Harmonic Bounds

1. Lower Bound via Left Riemann Sum

On each unit interval $[k, k+1]$, the function $f(x) = 1/x$ is strictly decreasing. The rectangle with height equal to the left endpoint $f(k) = 1/k$ completely covers the region under the curve:

$$ \frac{1}{k} > \int_{k}^{k+1} \frac{1}{x} dx $$

Summing from $k=1$ to $n$:

$$ H_n = \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{k} > \int_1^{n+1} \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln(n+1) $$

2. Upper Bound via Right Riemann Sum

Shift the staircase left by 1. Now on $[k-1, k]$, the rectangle of height $1/k$ uses the right endpoint, sitting strictly underneath $1/x$:

$$ \frac{1}{k} < \int_{k-1}^k \frac{1}{x} dx \quad (\text{for } k \ge 2) $$

Summing from $k=2$ to $n$ and adding the first block of area $1$:

$$ H_n - 1 = \sum_{k=2}^n \frac{1}{k} < \int_1^n \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln n $$ $$ \implies H_n < 1 + \ln n $$

Conclusion: Asymptotic Growth & The Euler Constant

Combining our geometric sandwich bounds proves that the harmonic sum is trapped tightly around $\ln n$:

$$ \ln(n+1) < H_n < 1 + \ln n $$

Because the difference $(1 + \ln n) - \ln(n+1) = 1 - \ln(1 + 1/n) \to 1$ as $n \to \infty$, the sequence $H_n - \ln n$ is monotonically decreasing and bounded below, converging to the famous Euler-Mascheroni constant: $$ \gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} (H_n - \ln n) \approx 0.5772156649\dots $$
