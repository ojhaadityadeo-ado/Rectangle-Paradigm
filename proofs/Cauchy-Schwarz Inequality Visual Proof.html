The Geometric Proof

1. Geometric Setup & Area Calculations

Left Figure: Bounding Rectangle
  • Dimensions: The outer rectangle has width $(|a| + |y|)$ and height $(|b| + |x|)$.
  • Total Area:
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{left}} = (|a| + |y|)(|b| + |x|)$$
  • Internal Breakdown: It is partitioned into:
    • Two dark gray right triangles with legs $|a|$ and $|b|$ $\left(\text{area } = \frac{1}{2}|a||b| \text{ each}\right)$.
    • Two dotted right triangles with legs $|x|$ and $|y|$ $\left(\text{area } = \frac{1}{2}|x||y| \text{ each}\right)$.
    • A central parallelogram whose adjacent sides are the hypotenuses of the right triangles: $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.
Right Figure: Composite Shape
  • Central Region: A rectangle with side lengths $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.
  • Attached Shapes: The exact same four right-angled triangles from the left figure are attached along the outer edges of this rectangle.
  • Total Area:
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{right}} = 2\left(\frac{1}{2}|a||b|\right) + 2\left(\frac{1}{2}|x||y|\right) + \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$

2. Geometric Reason for the Inequality ($\text{Area}_{\text{left}} \le \text{Area}_{\text{right}}$)

To understand why $\text{Area}_{\text{left}} \le \text{Area}_{\text{right}}$, notice that both figures contain the exact same four corner triangles ($2 \times \text{dark gray} + 2 \times \text{dotted}$).

Therefore, comparing the total areas of the two figures reduces to comparing their central shapes:

  • Left Central Shape: A parallelogram with sides $L_1 = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $L_2 = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Its area is:
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{parallelogram}} = L_1 L_2 \sin\theta$$
  • Right Central Shape: A rectangle with the same side lengths $L_1$ and $L_2$. Its area is:
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{rectangle}} = L_1 L_2$$

Because $\sin\theta \le 1$ for any angle $\theta$, the area of a parallelogram cannot exceed the area of a rectangle with the same side lengths:

$$\text{Area}_{\text{parallelogram}} \le \text{Area}_{\text{rectangle}} \implies \text{Area}_{\text{left}} \le \text{Area}_{\text{right}}$$

3. Step-by-Step Algebraic Proof

Step 1: Write the Area Inequality

From the geometric comparison:

$$(|a| + |y|)(|b| + |x|) \le 2\left(\frac{1}{2}|a||b|\right) + 2\left(\frac{1}{2}|x||y|\right) + \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$
Step 2: Expand Both Sides

Expanding the left-hand side and simplifying the right-hand side gives:

$$|a||b| + |a||x| + |y||b| + |y||x| \le |a||b| + |x||y| + \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$
Step 3: Cancel Common Terms

Subtract $|a||b| + |x||y|$ from both sides:

$$|a||x| + |b||y| \le \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$
Step 4: Apply the Triangle Inequality

By the real number triangle inequality, $|ax + by| \le |ax| + |by| = |a||x| + |b||y|$. Combining this yields the complete chain of inequalities:

$$|ax + by| \le |a||x| + |b||y| \le \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$
In vector notation, with $\mathbf{u} = \langle a, b \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle x, y \rangle$:
$$|\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}| \le \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\|$$
