Deriving the Algebraic Identity

1. Geometric Construction

Given a rectangle with sides $x$ and $y$ (where $x \ge y$), its perimeter is $P = 2x + 2y$.

A square with the exact same perimeter must have a side length of $s = \frac{x+y}{2}$. We want to compare the area of the rectangle ($xy$) to the area of the square ($s^2$).

To visualize this, we define a "difference" variable $\delta = \frac{x-y}{2}$. Notice that:

  • Width $x = s + \delta$
  • Height $y = s - \delta$

2. Tracking the Areas

As seen in the animation, the original rectangle is partitioned into a base and a cut piece. When rearranged into an L-shape, its area remains exactly $xy$.

The bounding square of side $s$ completely encapsulates this L-shape, but leaves a missing square gap in the corner. The dimensions of this missing gap are precisely $\delta \times \delta$.

$$\text{Square Area} = \text{Rectangle Area} + \text{Gap Area}$$ $$ \left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2 = x \cdot y + \left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)^2 $$

Conclusion: Isoperimetric Inequality

Because the area of the gap $\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)^2$ must always be $\ge 0$ (since any real number squared is non-negative), it follows directly that:

$$ \left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2 \ge x \cdot y $$

Furthermore, the area of the square is strictly greater than the rectangle unless the gap area is zero. The gap area is zero if and only if $\frac{x-y}{2} = 0$, which implies $x = y$. Thus, among all rectangles of a fixed perimeter, the square uniquely maximizes the enclosed area.
