Visual Proof: The Product Rule

Viewing the derivative of a product as the change in the area of an expanding rectangle.

$\frac{d}{dx}(u \cdot v) =$ $u \frac{dv}{dx}$ $+$ $v \frac{du}{dx}$

Proof Progression

1. The Setup (Initial Area) Imagine a rectangle representing a function $A = u \cdot v$. Its initial area is simply width $\times$ height.
2. The Perturbation We increase the input variable $x$ by $dx$, causing the sides to grow by tiny amounts $du$ and $dv$.
3. The First Strip The area grows by a vertical strip on the right. Its area is the old height $v$ times the new width change $du$.
4. The Second Strip The area also grows by a horizontal strip on the top. Its area is the old width $u$ times the new height change $dv$.
5. The Corner Square This leaves a tiny corner piece of area $du \cdot dv$.
6. The Limit as $dx \to 0$ As the changes become infinitesimally small, the corner piece $du \cdot dv$ vanishes much faster than the strips, leaving only the two main rectangles.

Deriving the Algebraic Identity

1. The Change in Area

When both functions $u$ and $v$ increase by a tiny amount, the new total area of the expanded rectangle is:

$$ A_{\text{new}} = (u + \Delta u)(v + \Delta v) $$

Expanding this algebraic product perfectly matches our geometric visual:

$$ A_{\text{new}} = u \cdot v + u \cdot \Delta v + v \cdot \Delta u + \Delta u \cdot \Delta v $$

2. Applying the Limit

The change in area $\Delta A$ is the new area minus the original $u \cdot v$:

$$ \Delta A = u \cdot \Delta v + v \cdot \Delta u + \Delta u \cdot \Delta v $$

To find the derivative, we divide the change in area by the change in our independent variable $x$, and take the limit as $\Delta x \to 0$:

$$ \frac{dA}{dx} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \left( u \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta x} + v \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta x} + \Delta u \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta x} \right) $$

Conclusion: The Vanishing Corner

As $\Delta x$ approaches zero, $\frac{\Delta u}{\Delta x}$ and $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta x}$ converge to finite derivatives $\frac{du}{dx}$ and $\frac{dv}{dx}$. However, the standalone $\Delta u$ term shrinks to exactly $0$. Geometrically, this means the red corner square shrinks much faster than the two rectangular strips.

$$ \frac{d}{dx}(u \cdot v) = u \frac{dv}{dx} + v \frac{du}{dx} $$

This leaves us with the fundamental Product Rule of calculus, beautifully derived from the changing geometry of a simple rectangle.
