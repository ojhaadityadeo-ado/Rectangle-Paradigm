Visual Proof: Area of a Right Triangle

$$\text{Area} = A$$
Step: 0 / 6

Consider a right-angled triangle. Let its area be $A$.

How this Proof Works

This visual proof elegantly demonstrates that the area $A$ of a right triangle is exactly equal to $x \cdot y$, where $x$ and $y$ are the lengths of the segments of the hypotenuse split by the incircle's point of tangency.

  1. Duplicate to form a Rectangle: By duplicating the triangle, we form a rectangle. Since it is made of two identical triangles, its total area is $2A$.
  2. Identify Components: The parts of the duplicate triangle can be rearranged along the edges to perfectly cover an area exactly equal to $A$ (because the original triangle's area consists of those exact border pieces derived from the inradius $r$).
  3. Observe the Remainder: Since the full rectangle is made of two triangles ($2A$), and we rearranged pieces covering exactly $A$ along the borders, the remaining empty space must equal the area of one original triangle.
  4. Conclusion: Because this remaining empty space is a clear rectangle with dimensions $x$ and $y$, its area is simply $x \cdot y$.
$$\text{Area of Right Triangle } A = x \cdot y$$
