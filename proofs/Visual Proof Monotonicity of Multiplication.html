Proof Progression

1. The Premise We establish three positive numbers where length $a$ is strictly less than length $b$, and they share a common positive multiplier $c$.
2. First Product ($ac$) Multiplying $a$ by $c$ creates a rectangle of area $ac$.
3. Second Product ($bc$) Multiplying $b$ by $c$ creates a wider rectangle of area $bc$. Notice how it encompasses the first.
4. Geometric Nesting Because $a < b$, the $ac$ rectangle fits strictly inside the $bc$ rectangle. The physical gap visually proves the difference.
5. The Conclusion The leftover area is $(b-a)c > 0$. Therefore, $ac$ is strictly less than $bc$.

Deriving the Monotonicity Property

1. Geometric Nesting of Areas

When we say $a < b$, geometrically we mean that a line segment of length $a$ is strictly shorter than a segment of length $b$.

Multiplying these lengths by a positive value $c$ extrudes both segments into two-dimensional rectangles that share the exact same height.

$$\text{Area}_1 = ac \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Area}_2 = bc$$

Because they share the same height $c$ and start at the same origin, the rectangle $ac$ is a strict subset of the rectangle $bc$.

2. Algebraic Factorization

Algebraically, if $a < b$, then their difference is positive: $b - a > 0$.

Because the multiplier $c$ is also strictly positive ($c > 0$), the product of two positive numbers must be positive:

$$(b - a) \cdot c > 0$$

Distributing $c$ through the parentheses yields $bc - ac > 0$, which is the exact mathematical equivalent of our physical "extra area" gap.

Conclusion: Preservation of Inequality

By showing that $bc - ac > 0$, we simply add $ac$ to both sides of the inequality to arrive at our final theorem. Because the extra area geometrically exists, the larger rectangle must contain more surface area than the smaller one:

$$ac < bc$$

Note: If $c$ were negative, the geometric extrusion would go in the opposite direction, flipping the logical orientation and reversing the inequality sign.
