Visual Proof: Perimeter × Inradius = 2 × Area

$$2 \times \text{Area} = r \times (a + b + c)$$
Step: 0 / 5

Consider a standard triangle with side lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$. Let its area be $A$.

Connecting Geometry to the Formula

This geometric dissection provides a stunning visual intuition for the relationship between a triangle's area ($A$), its perimeter ($P$), and the radius of its inscribed circle ($r$).

  1. Identify the Sub-Triangles: By drawing lines from the incenter to the vertices and dropping perpendiculars (the inradii, $r$) to the sides, the triangle splits into 6 smaller right-angled triangles.
  2. Duplication ($2A$): Because we want to solve for the Area elegantly, we duplicate the entire shape. We now have 12 right triangles representing a total area of $2A$.
  3. Tiling the Rectangle: Due to the properties of tangent lines to a circle, these 12 triangles consist of congruent pairs. We can perfectly unfold and interlock them to form a single continuous rectangle.
  4. Conclusion: The newly formed rectangle has a height exactly equal to the inradius $r$, and a width perfectly spanning the full perimeter $a + b + c$. Thus, the area of the rectangle is $r \times (a+b+c)$, which must equal $2A$.
$$A = \frac{1}{2}r(a + b + c)$$
