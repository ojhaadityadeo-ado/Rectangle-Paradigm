Visual Proof: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^n = \frac{1}{4}$

$$\frac{1}{5} + \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^3 + \dots = \frac{1}{4}$$

How to understand this proof:

  1. Start with a unit square: The outer square has an area of $1$.
  2. First Iteration ($n=1$): By drawing lines from each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side, we partition the square into $4$ identical right triangles and $1$ central tilted square. Each right triangle has an area of exactly $\frac{1}{5}$.
  3. Recursive Tiling ($n \to \infty$): We repeat this exact slicing process inside the central square. This produces $4$ smaller triangles of area $\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^2$. Continuing this process indefinitely forms $4$ identical, non-overlapping spiral "arms".
  4. The Final Sum: Together, these $4$ symmetric arms completely tile the unit square (area $1$) without leaving any gaps. Therefore, the area of a single arm (represented by the sum of its triangles) must equal exactly one-quarter of the total area:
$$\frac{1}{5} + \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^3 + \dots = \frac{1}{4}$$
