Proof Progression

1. The Initial Areas We construct a large square of area $(x+y)^2$ and a smaller square of area $(x-y)^2$. The total physical area is their sum.
2. Partition $(x+y)^2$ The $(x+y)^2$ square is naturally composed of an $x^2$ square, a $y^2$ square, and two $xy$ rectangles.
3. Slice the Rectangle We slice the top-right $xy$ rectangle into a smaller $y^2$ square and a remaining $y(x-y)$ strip.
4. Geometric Reassembly We move these sliced pieces over to the $(x-y)^2$ square. They perfectly interlock to form a brand new $x^2$ square!
5. The Conclusion The total area has been reassembled into exactly two $x^2$ squares and two $y^2$ squares. Thus, $(x+y)^2 \le 2(x^2 + y^2)$.

Deriving the Algebraic Property

1. Adding the Squares

Let's evaluate the sum of the physical areas we started with in the visual proof:

$$ \text{Total Area} = (x+y)^2 + (x-y)^2 $$

By expanding both binomials algebraically, we get: $$ (x^2 + 2xy + y^2) + (x^2 - 2xy + y^2) $$

2. Cancellation

The positive cross-term $+2xy$ and the negative cross-term $-2xy$ perfectly cancel each other out. This leaves us with:

$$ x^2 + y^2 + x^2 + y^2 = 2x^2 + 2y^2 $$

This algebraic result exactly matches our geometric reassembly, where the area was transformed into exactly two $x^2$ squares and two $y^2$ squares.

Conclusion: The Inequality

We have proven that $(x+y)^2 + (x-y)^2 = 2(x^2 + y^2)$.

Because any real number squared is non-negative, the physical area of the $(x-y)^2$ square must be greater than or equal to zero.

$$ 2(x^2 + y^2) - (x+y)^2 = (x-y)^2 \ge 0 $$

By simply adding $(x+y)^2$ to both sides, we arrive at our fundamental inequality: $2(x^2 + y^2) \ge (x+y)^2$, with equality holding only when $x=y$.
