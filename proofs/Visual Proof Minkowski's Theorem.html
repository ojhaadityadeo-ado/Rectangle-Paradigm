Proof Progression

1. The Convex Body Consider a convex shape $S$ that is symmetric about the origin $(0,0)$. We establish that its total Area is strictly $> 4$.
2. The $2 \times 2$ Grid Overlay a grid of $2 \times 2$ squares onto the plane. Note that each single square contains exactly an area of $4$.
3. Cut and Translate Cut the shape along the grid lines. Translate all the resulting pieces by even integers so they stack into the central $2 \times 2$ square.
4. Pigeonhole Principle Because the sum of the pieces' areas is $>4$, but they are packed into a box of Area exactly $4$, the Continuous Pigeonhole Principle dictates they must overlap at some point $P$.
5. Mapping Back Tracing $P$ back to its un-translated origins, we find two distinct points $A$ and $B$ inside $S$. Because they translated to the same spot, their difference is exactly an even integer vector: $A - B = (2m, 2n)$.
6. Symmetry & Convexity Since $S$ is symmetric, if $B \in S$, then $-B \in S$. Because $S$ is convex, the midpoint of $A$ and $-B$ must also be in $S$. This midpoint is $(A - B)/2 = (m, n)$, which is a non-zero integer lattice point!

Continuous Pigeonhole & Convexity

1. The Pigeonhole Principle for Area

The classic Pigeonhole Principle states that if you put $N+1$ pigeons into $N$ holes, at least one hole has multiple pigeons. This naturally extends to continuous area.

If you take physical pieces of paper with a combined area strictly greater than $4$, and force them to lay flat entirely inside a $2 \times 2$ bounding box (which has area exactly $4$), it is physically impossible to do so without at least two pieces overlapping.

$$ \text{Total Area} > \text{Container Area} \implies \text{Overlap} $$

2. The Midpoint Trick

Once we establish the overlap exists, the theorem is purely algebraic geometry. We know $A \in S$ and $B \in S$, and $A - B = (2m, 2n)$.

  • Symmetry: If $B \in S$, then $-B \in S$.
  • Convexity: The line segment connecting any two points in $S$ is entirely inside $S$. Thus, the midpoint of $A$ and $-B$ is in $S$.
$$ \text{Midpoint} = \frac{A + (-B)}{2} = \frac{A - B}{2} = (m, n) $$

Because $m$ and $n$ are integers, $(m,n)$ is a lattice point. Because $A \neq B$, the point $(m,n) \neq (0,0)$.
