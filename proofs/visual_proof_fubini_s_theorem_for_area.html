Fubini's Theorem for Area

Computing the exact same 2D area by slicing it into vertical strips (integrating $y$ then $x$) versus horizontal strips (integrating $x$ then $y$).

$$ \text{Area}(D) = \iint_D 1 \, dA $$

Proof Progression

1. The 2D Region $D$ Consider the area bounded between the line $y = \frac{x}{2}$ and the parabola $y = \frac{x^2}{8}$. We want to find the exact area of Region $D$.
2. Inner Integral (Fix $x$, Integrate $y$) Fix an $x$-coordinate. The vertical distance between the curves forms a strip of height $(\frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{8})$ and thickness $dx$.
3. Outer Integral (Sweep $x$) By sweeping these vertical strips across the $x$-axis from $0$ to $4$, we accumulate the total area: $\int_0^4 \left( \int_{x^2/8}^{x/2} 1 \, dy \right) dx$.
4. Inner Integral (Fix $y$, Integrate $x$) Alternatively, fix a $y$-coordinate. The horizontal distance between the curves forms a strip of width $(\sqrt{8y} - 2y)$ and thickness $dy$.
5. Outer Integral (Sweep $y$) By sweeping these horizontal strips up the $y$-axis from $0$ to $2$, we also accumulate the exact same total area: $\int_0^2 \left( \int_{2y}^{\sqrt{8y}} 1 \, dx \right) dy$.
6. Fubini's Conclusion Because both slicing methods perfectly tile the exact same region $D$, the order of integration is interchangeable. Both evaluate to $\frac{4}{3}$!

Mathematical Analysis

1. Vertical Slicing (Type I Region)

When we treat the shape as a Type I region, we integrate with respect to $y$ first. The lower boundary is $y = x^2/8$ and the upper boundary is $y = x/2$.

$$ \int_0^4 \left[ \int_{x^2/8}^{x/2} 1 \, dy \right] dx $$

Evaluating the inner integral yields $(x/2 - x^2/8)$. The outer integral then computes $\int_0^4 (\frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{8}) dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{x^3}{24}\right]_0^4 = 4 - \frac{64}{24} = \frac{4}{3}$.

2. Horizontal Slicing (Type II Region)

To treat it as a Type II region, we must invert the functions to solve for $x$. The left boundary becomes $x = 2y$ and the right boundary becomes $x = \sqrt{8y}$.

$$ \int_0^2 \left[ \int_{2y}^{\sqrt{8y}} 1 \, dx \right] dy $$

Evaluating the inner integral yields $(\sqrt{8y} - 2y)$. The outer integral computes $\int_0^2 (\sqrt{8}y^{1/2} - 2y) dy = \left[\sqrt{8}\frac{2}{3}y^{3/2} - y^2\right]_0^2 = \frac{16}{3} - 4 = \frac{4}{3}$.
