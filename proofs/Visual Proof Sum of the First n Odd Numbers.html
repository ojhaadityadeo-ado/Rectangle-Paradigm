The Geometric Insight: Gnomons

To find the sum of the first $n$ odd numbers ($1 + 3 + 5 + \dots + 2n-1$), we can represent each odd number as an L-shaped block of unit squares. In geometry, this L-shaped border is called a gnomon.

Notice how the $k$-th odd number is precisely the number of blocks needed to wrap around an existing $(k-1) \times (k-1)$ square to transform it into a larger $k \times k$ square.

Algebraic Connection

The difference in area between a square of side $k$ and a square of side $k-1$ is exactly the $k$-th odd number:

$k^2 - (k-1)^2$
$= k^2 - (k^2 - 2k + 1)$
$= 2k - 1$

Because stacking the first $n$ odd numbers perfectly tiles a solid $n \times n$ square, their total sum must exactly equal the area of that square ($n^2$).

$$ \sum_{i=1}^{n} (2i-1) = n^2 $$
