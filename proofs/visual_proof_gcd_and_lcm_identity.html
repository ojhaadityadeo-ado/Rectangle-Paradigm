Proof Progression

1. The Original Area We start with a rectangle of width $a$ and height $b$. The total geometric area is exactly $a \times b$.
2. Identifying the Blocks We find the greatest common divisor $g = \gcd(a,b)$. We perfectly divide the rectangle into a grid of smaller square blocks, each measuring $g \times g$. We get $x = a/g$ columns and $y = b/g$ rows.
3. Rearranging the Area Because these blocks represent discrete, invariant units of area, we can physically rearrange them without adding or losing any overall space. Let's line them all up.
4. The New Dimensions We align all the blocks into a single continuous row. The new height is exactly $g$. The new length is made of $N = x \cdot y$ blocks of width $g$, which is exactly $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$. Area is conserved!

Mathematical Foundation

1. Area Decomposition

Let $g = \gcd(a, b)$. By definition, $g$ perfectly divides both $a$ and $b$. We can express our side lengths as $a = g \cdot x$ and $b = g \cdot y$, where $x$ and $y$ are coprime integers.

Substituting these into the area formula of the rectangle:

$$ \text{Area} = a \times b = (g \cdot x) \times (g \cdot y) $$

This mathematically proves that the area can be cleanly divided into a grid of $x \times y$ smaller square blocks, each with an area of $g^2$.

2. The LCM Construction

The total number of $g \times g$ blocks is $N = x \cdot y$. If we lay them end-to-end, the total length is $N \cdot g = x \cdot y \cdot g$.

Notice that the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of $a$ and $b$ is the smallest multiple of both. Since $a = gx$ and $b = gy$, their LCM is precisely $g \cdot x \cdot y$.

$$ \text{Length} = N \cdot g = (x \cdot y) \cdot g = \operatorname{lcm}(a, b) $$

Conclusion: Conservation of Area

Because we simply rearranged physical blocks of area, the total area of the final $g \times \operatorname{lcm}(a,b)$ strip must equal the total area of the original $a \times b$ rectangle. This provides a rigorous, visual proof of the identity:

$$ \gcd(a, b) \times \operatorname{lcm}(a, b) = a \times b $$
