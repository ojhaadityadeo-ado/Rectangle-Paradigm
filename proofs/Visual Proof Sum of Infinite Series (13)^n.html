How to understand this proof:

  1. Start with a unit rectangle: The outer rectangle has a total area of $1$.
  2. Step 1 ($n=1$): Divide the rectangle vertically into $3$ equal columns. Shade the left column ($\frac{1}{3}$) in rust red for our series. Notice that we intentionally leave the right column ($\frac{1}{3}$) blank (white) as a "balance".
  3. Step 2 ($n=2$): Divide the remaining center rectangle horizontally into $3$ equal rows. Shade the top row ($\frac{1}{9}$) and leave the bottom row blank ($\frac{1}{9}$).
  4. Spiral Recursion ($n \to \infty$): We repeat this $1/3$ division continuously in a spiral order (Left $\to$ Top $\to$ Right $\to$ Bottom). At every single step $n$, for every shaded block of area $\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n$ added to the series, an identical unshaded block of area $\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^n$ is left blank.
  5. The Final Limit: Since every shaded piece is paired with an identical unshaded piece, the total shaded area equals the unshaded area: $$\text{Shaded Area} = \text{Unshaded Area}$$ Because together they completely fill the outer rectangle of area $1$, the infinite sum of shaded pieces must equal exactly half:
$$\frac{1}{3} + \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3 + \dots = \frac{1}{2}$$
