The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus as Growing Area

Define \(A(x)\) to be the area accumulated under \(f\) from a fixed starting point to \(x\). When \(x\) moves a tiny distance \(h\), the new area is almost a rectangle of height \(f(x)\). That is why the derivative of accumulated area is the original function.

\[ \boxed{\displaystyle A(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A'(x)=f(x)} \]

The visual proof of FTC Part I

Define accumulated area. Let \[ A(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt. \] This means \(A(x)\) is the area under the graph from the fixed point \(a\) to the moving endpoint \(x\).
Move the endpoint from \(x\) to \(x+h\). The change in accumulated area is \[ \Delta A=A(x+h)-A(x)=\int_x^{x+h}f(t)\,dt. \]
Compare the new sliver with rectangles. If \(f\) is increasing on this tiny interval, then \[ f(x)h\le \Delta A\le f(x+h)h. \]
Divide by the width \(h\). This gives \[ f(x)\le \frac{\Delta A}{h}\le f(x+h). \] The middle quantity is the average height of the new sliver.
Let \(h\to0\). Continuity makes \(f(x+h)\to f(x)\), so both bounds squeeze the average height to \(f(x)\): \[ A'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{A(x+h)-A(x)}{h}=f(x). \]

The intuition in one sentence

\[ \boxed{\text{rate at which area grows}=\text{current height of the graph}} \]

When you increase \(x\) by a tiny amount \(dx\), you add a very thin strip whose width is \(dx\) and whose height is approximately \(f(x)\). Therefore

\[ dA\approx f(x)\,dx \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{dA}{dx}=f(x). \]

FTC Part II: why antiderivatives evaluate definite integrals

From Part I, the accumulation function \[ A(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt \] is an antiderivative of \(f\). So if \(F'(x)=f(x)\), then \(F\) and \(A\) differ only by a constant:

\[ A(x)=F(x)-F(a). \]

Now set \(x=b\):

\[ \boxed{\displaystyle \int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)} \]

The example used in the animation

The curve in the interactive graphic is \[ f(x)=1+\frac{2}{5}x^2. \] One antiderivative is \[ F(x)=x+\frac{2}{15}x^3. \] Therefore

\[ \int_0^b \left(1+\frac25x^2\right)\,dx = b+\frac{2}{15}b^3. \]

The sliders let you see the difference quotient \[ \frac{A(x+h)-A(x)}{h} \] move closer to \(f(x)\) as \(h\) becomes smaller.
