Deriving the Inequality

The Rearrangement Inequality states that when we multiply pairs from two sorted sequences, the sum is maximized when they are sorted in the same order (largest with largest), and minimized when sorted in opposite order. Here is why it works visually for two pairs.

1. Two Ways to Pair

Assume $x_1 \le x_2$ and $y_1 \le y_2$. The four possible areas are $x_1y_1$, $x_1y_2$, $x_2y_1$, and $x_2y_2$.

Pairing "like with like" gives the sorted sum; pairing them backwards gives the mismatched sum:

Mismatched = $x_1y_2 + x_2y_1$
Sorted = $x_1y_1 + x_2y_2$

2. The Gained Difference

If we subtract the Mismatched area from the Sorted area, the terms factor perfectly:

$$ (x_1y_1 + x_2y_2) - (x_1y_2 + x_2y_1) = (x_2-x_1)(y_2-y_1) $$

Because $x_2 \ge x_1$ and $y_2 \ge y_1$, both factors are non-negative. This product represents the physical area of the top-right quadrant.

Since this "gained area" must be $\ge 0$, the Sorted Sum is always greater than or equal to the Mismatched Sum.
