Tens and Ones: See Why 23 Means 2 Tens and 3 Ones

A two-digit number is not just two symbols. It tells us how many full groups of ten we have, and how many extra ones are left. This page turns the number into tiles, bundles the tiles into tens, and shows the place-value sentence in a way small children can see and understand.

How this helps a child understand place value

See the number as objects. First the child sees the number as separate little squares. This tells them that the number is a quantity.
Make groups of ten. Instead of counting forever one by one, we make full groups of 10. Each full group becomes one “ten”.
Notice what is left over. After the full groups of 10 are made, any extra little squares are called “ones”.
Read the digits correctly. In a number like 23, the 2 tells how many tens, and the 3 tells how many ones.
Connect picture and number sentence. The final statement is:
23 = 20 + 3 = 2 tens and 3 ones.

Why the tens digit is powerful

One little square is worth 1. But one full row of 10 little squares is worth 10. So the digit in the tens place is much stronger than the digit in the ones place.
