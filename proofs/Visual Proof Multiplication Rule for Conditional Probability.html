Proof Progression

1. The Sample Space ($\Omega$) We start with a $1 \times 1$ unit square. The total area equals $1$.
2. Event $A$ Slice the square vertically to form a strip of width $P(A)$. The area of this entire strip is $P(A) \times 1 = P(A)$.
3. The Condition ($B \mid A$) Within the strip $A$, we look for event $B$. Event $B$ occupies a vertical fraction equal to $P(B \mid A)$.
4. The Intersection ($A \cap B$) The resulting shaded rectangle is the intersection $A \cap B$, meaning both events occurred.
5. The Area Calculation The area of this final rectangle is exactly its Width $\times$ Height: $P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A)$.

Deriving the Algebraic Property

1. Restricting the Sample Space

When we calculate $P(A)$, we evaluate the area of $A$ relative to the entire $1 \times 1$ sample space $\Omega$. This is represented by a vertical rectangle of width $P(A)$.

$$ \text{Area}(A) = P(A) \times 1 = P(A) $$

For conditional probability, we "restrict" our focus completely to this new sub-region $A$. We no longer care about anything outside the vertical strip.

2. Height as Conditional Probability

Inside the restricted universe of $A$, the event $B$ occurs with a relative probability of $P(B \mid A)$. Geometrically, this means $B$ fills up a vertical fraction of $A$ equal to $P(B \mid A)$.

$$ \text{Height of } B \text{ within } A = P(B \mid A) $$

Because the intersection $A \cap B$ is simply the geometric region where both events overlap, its physical size is a rectangle bounded by width $P(A)$ and height $P(B \mid A)$.

Conclusion: The Multiplication Rule

By formally taking the area of the highlighted sub-rectangle (Width $\times$ Height), we geometrically construct the Multiplication Rule for Conditional Probability:

$$ P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A) $$

This visual proof intuitively confirms that the probability of $A$ and $B$ both happening is the probability of $A$ happening, scaled by the fraction of $A$ where $B$ also occurs.
