Visual Proof: Sine Addition Formula

Resolving a unit direction into components relative to $\alpha$, then projecting those rectangular components vertically.

$$ \sin(\alpha+\beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta $$
Angle α 30°
Angle β 25°
Total Angle α+β 55°
Step: 0 / 4

Proof Progression

1. The Target Height Take a hypotenuse $OR$ of length 1 rotated by $(\alpha+\beta)$. Dropping a perpendicular to the x-axis yields $RS$, whose exact vertical height is $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$.
2. Resolving the Components Resolve the length-1 direction first into components relative to angle $\alpha$. Drop a perpendicular from $R$ to the ray of angle $\alpha$ to find $P$. Now $OP = \cos \beta$ and $PR = \sin \beta$.
3. Projecting the Base Component Project the $OP$ component vertically. Drop a perpendicular from $P$ to the x-axis ($Q$). Since $OP$ is at angle $\alpha$ and has length $\cos \beta$, its vertical projection is $PQ = \sin \alpha \cos \beta$.
4. Projecting the Orthogonal Component Project the $PR$ component vertically. Draw a horizontal from $P$ to intersect $RS$ at $T$. Because $PR \perp OP$, the angle $\angle PRT = \alpha$. Thus, the vertical projection $RT$ is $\cos \alpha \sin \beta$.
5. Recombining the Projections The total vertical height $RS$ is exactly the sum of its two vertical projection contributions: $RT + TS$. Since $TS = PQ$, we get the full addition formula!

Trigonometric Projections Explained

1. Resolving the Angle

The key to unlocking this proof is identifying that the angle $\angle PRT$ at the very top is exactly equal to angle $\alpha$. How do we know this geometrically?

  • The line $PT$ is completely horizontal, and line $RS$ is completely vertical, meaning $\angle PTR = 90^\circ$.
  • The ray $OP$ is angled at $\alpha$ from the horizontal. Thus, the angle of $OP$ to the vertical is $90^\circ - \alpha$.
  • Since $PR \perp OP$ ($\angle OPR = 90^\circ$), the segment $PR$ is tilted exactly angle $\alpha$ away from the vertical line segment $TR$. Therefore, in the right triangle $\triangle PTR$, the top angle $\angle PRT$ must be $\alpha$.

2. Summing Vertical Projections

Once we resolve the unit direction into components relative to $\alpha$ ($OP$ and $PR$), the total height is simply the sum of their individual vertical projections onto the y-axis.

$$ RS = RT + TS $$

Substituting the values derived from our reference triangles:

$RS = \sin(\alpha+\beta)$ (from the large triangle $ORS$)
$RT = \cos\alpha \sin\beta$ (vertical projection of component $PR$)
$TS = PQ = \sin\alpha \cos\beta$ (vertical projection of component $OP$)
