The Geometric Proof

Let $T_k = 1 + 2 + \dots + k = \frac{k(k+1)}{2}$ be the $k$-th triangular number. We build a square of side length $T_n$ one L-shaped "gnomon" at a time, and show that the $k$-th gnomon always has an area of exactly $k^3$.

Suppose we already have a square of side $T_{k-1}$. Adding gnomon $k$ means attaching a $k \times k$ corner square plus two arms, each $T_{k-1} \times k$, along the top and side.

The Gnomon's Area is a Perfect Cube

The geometry tells us the area of the $k$-th gnomon is:

$$ \text{Area} = k^2 + 2 \cdot k \cdot T_{k-1} $$

Substituting the formula for $T_{k-1}$:

$$ = k^2 + 2k \cdot \frac{(k-1)k}{2} $$

$$ = k^2 + k^2(k-1) = k^2 \cdot k = k^3 $$

So each colored layer in the diagram has an area of exactly $k^3$.

Stacking gnomons $1$ through $n$ builds up, layer by layer, one full square of side $T_n$. Since the gnomons' areas sum to $\sum k^3$ and the square's area is $T_n^2$, those two quantities must be equal:

$$ 1^3 + 2^3 + \dots + n^3 = (1+2+\dots+n)^2 $$
