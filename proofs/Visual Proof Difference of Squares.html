How to understand this proof:

  1. Start with a large square: We begin with a square of side length $a$. Its total area is $a \times a = a^2$.
  2. Remove a smaller square: We cut out a small square of side length $b$ (area $b^2$) from the corner. The remaining area is exactly $a^2 - b^2$.
  3. Rearrange the pieces: The remaining "L-shape" can be split into two distinct rectangles (blue and green). By moving and rotating the green rectangle, we can attach it perfectly to the bottom of the blue rectangle.
  4. The Final Result: The new combined shape is a perfect rectangle! Its height is $(a + b)$ and its width is $(a - b)$. Because we haven't added or removed any area since step 2, the area of this new rectangle must equal the area of the L-shape.

$a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$
