Proof Progression

1. The Integrand Curve Consider the function $f(t) = \frac{1}{1+t^2}$ on the interval $[0, x]$. Notice that it strictly decreases from a maximum of $1$ down to a minimum of $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$.
2. The Exact Area Integrating $f(t)$ from $0$ to $x$ yields the exact area under the curve. Mathematically, the anti-derivative evaluates precisely to $\arctan x$.
3. The Lower Rectangle Because the curve never drops below $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$, we can draw an inscribed rectangle of width $x$ and height $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$. Its area provides a strict lower bound.
4. The Upper Rectangle Because the curve never exceeds $1$, we can draw a circumscribed rectangle of width $x$ and height $1$. Its area is $x \times 1 = x$, providing a strict upper bound.
5. The Sandwich Inequality Geometrically nesting these areas immediately proves our fundamental inequality: $\frac{x}{1+x^2} \le \arctan x \le x$.

Mathematical Analysis

1. The Definite Integral

The true area of the region is defined by the definite integral of the function evaluated from $0$ to $x$:

$$ \int_0^x \frac{1}{1+t^2} \, dt = \Big[\arctan t\Big]_0^x = \arctan x - \arctan 0 = \arctan x $$

This integral property translates the physical area of the blue region into the exact value of the inverse tangent function.

2. Bounding the Integrand

On the interval $t \in [0, x]$ (assuming $x > 0$), the function $f(t) = \frac{1}{1+t^2}$ is strictly decreasing. Therefore, its minimum is at the right endpoint and its maximum is at the left endpoint:

$$ \frac{1}{1+x^2} \le \frac{1}{1+t^2} \le 1 $$

Because definite integration perfectly preserves inequalities, integrating all three parts from $0$ to $x$ immediately yields $\frac{x}{1+x^2} \le \arctan x \le x$.
