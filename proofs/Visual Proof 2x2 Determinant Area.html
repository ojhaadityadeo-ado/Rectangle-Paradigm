2×2 Determinant Area

A visual proof demonstrating how translating triangles reveals that the area of a parallelogram is exactly the determinant of its spanning vectors.

Area = $\det \begin{pmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc$
Vector 1 $\mathbf{u} = \langle a, b \rangle$
Vector 2 $\mathbf{v} = \langle c, d \rangle$
Step: 0 / 5

Proof Progression

1. The Target Parallelogram We want to find the area of the Parallelogram spanned by the two given vectors: $\mathbf{u} = \langle a, b \rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle c, d \rangle$.
2. The Bounding Rectangle We construct a large dashed bounding rectangle of width $a$ and height $d$. Its total area is trivially $\mathbf{ad}$.
3. Identifying the Leftovers The rectangle covers the core of our parallelogram but leaves two "leftover" triangles: T₁ (Top-Left) and T₂ (Bottom-Right).
4. Geometric Translation We slide T₁ along vector $\mathbf{u}$ and T₂ along vector $\mathbf{v}$. They move perfectly outside the box to complete the rest of the parallelogram.
5. The Overlap is bc The moved triangles overlap in the top-right corner. This cross-hatched region forms a perfect rectangle of width $c$ and height $b$. Its area is exactly $\mathbf{bc}$.
6. The Final Formula The translated triangles equal the missing outer pieces plus the overlap. Thus, Parallelogram Area = Bounding Rectangle ($ad$) - Overlap ($bc$).

Deriving the Determinant

1. Area Conservation

Notice that the area of the large bounding rectangle is completely accounted for by summing up internal regions:

$$ \text{Rect}(ad) = \text{Core} + T_1 + T_2 $$

Where $\text{Core}$ is the piece of the parallelogram sitting safely inside the box.

2. The Parallelogram's True Area

The true area of the Parallelogram is the Core plus the two missing outer tips. The animation proves that moving $T_1$ and $T_2$ perfectly covers these tips, but double-counts an overlapping rectangle of area $bc$.

$$ \text{Parallelogram} = \text{Core} + (T_1 + T_2) - bc $$

Substituting the first equation into the second yields the determinant formula: $\text{Area} = ad - bc$.
