Proof Progression

1. The Integer Partition An integer $n$ can be broken into a sum of decreasing integers. We represent this as a stack of $1 \times 1$ blocks called a Young Diagram.
2. Parts vs. Sizes The total number of rows defines the number of parts ($p$). The length of the longest row defines the maximum part size ($k$).
3. The Reflection Axis We draw a main diagonal line extending from the top-left corner. We will use this as a mirror.
4. Conjugation (The Flip) Reflect every single block physically across the diagonal. Every row becomes a column, and every column becomes a row!
5. Conservation of Area No blocks were added or destroyed; the total area $n$ is perfectly conserved. However, the diagram's dimensions have swapped perfectly.
6. The Bijection Theorem Because every valid diagram can be uniquely flipped, the number of partitions with at most $k$ parts must exactly equal the number of partitions with parts of size at most $k$.

Deriving the Theorem

1. Reading the Diagram

A partition of an integer $n$ is mathematically defined as a sequence of positive integers $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_p)$ such that:

$$ \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_p > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{p} \lambda_i = n $$

By representing each $\lambda_i$ as a horizontal row of $1 \times 1$ rectangular blocks, the total physical area strictly equals $n$. The number of rows is the number of parts.

2. The Bounding Rectangle & The Flip

Every Young Diagram fits tightly inside a bounding rectangle of width $k$ (the maximum part size $\lambda_1$) and height $p$ (the total number of parts).

Reflecting the area across the main diagonal defines the Conjugate Partition $\lambda'$. Mathematically, the $j$-th part of the conjugate is defined by counting vertical columns:

$$ \lambda'_j = |\{ i : \lambda_i \ge j \}| $$

Conclusion: Euler's Theorem

Because this geometric transformation is entirely reversible (a bijection) and flawlessly conserves the total area $n$, it guarantees a 1-to-1 mapping between two distinct sets of partitions.

$$ \text{Partitions with } \le k \text{ parts} \iff \text{Partitions with max part size } \le k $$

This elegant reasoning avoids complex algebraic generating functions, relying purely on the indisputable properties of 2D area conservation.
