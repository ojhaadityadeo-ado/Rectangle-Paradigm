Proof Progression

1. The Isosceles Triangle Consider a triangle with two sides of length $1$ and an included angle of $2\theta$. Its area is $\frac{1}{2}(1)(1)\sin(2\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)$.
2. Dropping the Altitude Drop an altitude from the top vertex. This perfectly bisects the angle into two angles of $\theta$, creating two congruent right triangles.
3. Right Triangle Dimensions Using standard trigonometry, the base of each right triangle is $\sin\theta$ and the altitude (height) is $\cos\theta$. The area of one right triangle is $\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta$.
4. The Area Equivalence Since the original triangle is made of two right triangles, its area is $2 \times (\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta) = \sin\theta\cos\theta$. Equating the two methods: $\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta) = \sin\theta\cos\theta$.
5. Doubling to Rectangles To remove the $\frac{1}{2}$ fraction, multiply everything by 2. Visually, we duplicate the two right triangles. They rotate to form two perfect $\sin\theta \times \cos\theta$ rectangles!
6. The Conclusion The total area of the two rectangles is $2\sin\theta\cos\theta$. Because they were formed by doubling the original triangle, their area exactly equals $\sin(2\theta)$.

Mathematical Analysis

1. Area of the Full Triangle

Using the standard formula for the area of a triangle given two sides and the included angle ($\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$), we evaluate our initial isosceles triangle:

$$ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(1)(1)\sin(2\theta) = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta) $$

This gives us a direct expression for the area involving the double angle $2\theta$.

2. Area by Decomposition

By dropping the altitude, we split the shape into two congruent right triangles. In each right triangle, the hypotenuse is $1$, so the base is $\sin\theta$ and the height is $\cos\theta$.

$$ \text{Total Area} = 2 \times \left( \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height} \right) = \sin\theta\cos\theta $$

Equating both area formulas yields $\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta) = \sin\theta\cos\theta$. Multiplying both sides by 2 produces the classic double angle identity!
