Visual Proof: Equivalent Fractions

Subdividing a rectangle to geometrically prove that multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same value preserves the shaded area.

$$ \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 3} = \frac{3}{6} $$

Proof Progression

1. The Base Fraction A rectangle is divided into $d$ equal vertical columns. We shade $n$ of them to represent the fraction $n/d$.
2. The Multiplier We choose to subdivide the entire shape. We will slice every single column into $m$ new horizontal pieces.
3. Subdividing the Whole By cutting the shape horizontally, the total number of pieces in the entire rectangle becomes $d \times m$. This is our new denominator.
4. The New Numerator Look at the shaded region. It was originally $n$ pieces, but because of the slicing, it is now made of $n \times m$ smaller pieces.
5. Conservation of Area The physical boundary of the shaded area hasn't changed at all! Since the area is conserved, the new fraction represents the exact same mathematical quantity.

Mathematical Analysis

1. Multiplying by One

The fundamental rule of equivalent fractions relies on the identity property of multiplication: multiplying any number by $1$ leaves it unchanged.

We represent the number $1$ as a fraction where the numerator and denominator are the same: $\frac{m}{m} = 1$. Therefore:

$$ \frac{n}{d} \times 1 = \frac{n}{d} \times \frac{m}{m} = \frac{n \times m}{d \times m} $$

2. The Geometric Translation

Our visual proof translates this algebra directly into geometry.

  • The denominator dictates the total number of sub-blocks forming the whole (Area = 1).
  • The numerator counts how many of those blocks are shaded.
  • By slicing the shape orthogonally (multiplying by $m$), every single block is split into $m$ smaller blocks. Both the total count and shaded count scale up by the exact same factor, maintaining the area ratio.
