Visual Proof: AM $\ge$ GM

$$\text{Arithmetic Mean} \ge \text{Geometric Mean} \implies \frac{x+y}{2} \ge \sqrt{xy}$$
Side x 7.0
Side y 3.0

Geometric Breakdown

1. The Outer Square

The side length is $x + y$. Therefore, the total area of the bounding box is exactly $(x+y)^2$.

100.00

2. The Four Inner Rectangles

We pack 4 congruent rectangles of size $x \times y$ in a pinwheel around the edges.

Area = 4xy 4(7.0)(3.0) = 84.00

3. Area Comparison

The 4 rectangles fit entirely inside the square, leaving an empty central gap of $(x-y)^2$. Therefore, their combined area must be $\le$ the square's area.

$4xy \le (x+y)^2$ 84.00 $\le$ 100.00

Deriving AM $\ge$ GM

The visual proves undeniably that the area of the four rectangles is less than or equal to the area of the large bounding square. We can write this geometrically as:

$$ 4xy \le (x+y)^2 $$

Equality holds strictly when the central gap vanishes. The area of the gap is $(y-x)^2$. This gap becomes $0$ exactly when $x = y$.

Algebraic Transformation

Since we assume $x$ and $y$ are non-negative, $x+y \ge 0$. We can safely take the square root of both sides of our inequality:

$$ \sqrt{4xy} \le \sqrt{(x+y)^2} $$

$$ 2\sqrt{xy} \le x+y $$

Dividing both sides by 2 isolates the Arithmetic Mean on the right and the Geometric Mean on the left:

$$ \sqrt{xy} \le \frac{x+y}{2} $$

The Arithmetic Mean (AM) is $\frac{x+y}{2}$ and the Geometric Mean (GM) is $\sqrt{xy}$. As seen visually, AM is always greater than or equal to GM.

$$ \text{AM} \ge \text{GM} $$
