Visual Proof: Area of a Parallelogram

$$\text{Area of Parallelogram} = b \times h$$

Start: We have a parallelogram with a horizontal base length of $b$ and a vertical height of $h$.

How this Geometric Proof Works:

  1. Drop a Perpendicular: We drop a straight vertical line from the top-left corner down to the base. This represents the height $h$.
  2. Cut the Parallelogram: Slicing along this height line detaches a right-angled triangular "sliver" (orange) from the left side.
  3. Slide to the Opposite Side: Because opposite sides of a parallelogram are perfectly parallel and equal in length, sliding this sliver exactly the distance of base $b$ allows it to fit perfectly into the empty space on the right.
  4. The Conclusion: Cutting and rearranging pieces does not change total area. The new shape is a plain rectangle with a width of $b$ and a height of $h$. Therefore, the area of the original parallelogram is exactly $b \times h$.
$$\text{Area of Parallelogram} = \text{Base} \times \text{Height} = b \times h$$
