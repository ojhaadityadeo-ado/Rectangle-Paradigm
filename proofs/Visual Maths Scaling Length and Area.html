When Lengths Scale, Area Scales Twice

If both side lengths of a rectangle are multiplied by the same scale factor, the area gets multiplied by that factor twice. So doubling both sides gives 4 times the area, and tripling both sides gives 9 times the area.

\(\text{lengths}\times2 \Rightarrow \text{area}\times4\)

What the picture means

The scale factor appears twice in area

The width is multiplied by 2 and the height is also multiplied by 2. Since area is width times height, the area receives two factors of 2.

Area calculation
\(A'= (2w)(2h)=4wh=4A\)
What is happening?
The larger rectangle can be split into 4 copies of the original rectangle. That is why doubling both sides makes the area 4 times as large.

How to explain scaling to small kids

Start with a rectangle. Suppose its width is \(w\) and its height is \(h\). Its area is \[ A=wh. \]
Stretch the width. If every length is multiplied by \(k\), the new width is \(kw\).
Stretch the height too. The new height is \(kh\).
Area uses both dimensions. The new area is \[ (kw)(kh)=k^2wh. \]
So area scales by \(k^2\). If \(k=2\), area multiplies by \(4\). If \(k=3\), area multiplies by \(9\).
\[ \boxed{\text{side lengths}\times k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{area}\times k^2} \]
