Proof Progression

1. The Target Triangle Consider a triangle defined by three coordinate vertices $V_1, V_2, V_3$. We want to find its exact area geometrically.
2. The Bounding Rectangle Enclose the triangle perfectly inside a bounding rectangle. The area of the rectangle is simply $\text{width} \times \text{height} = (x_2 - x_1)(y_3 - y_1)$.
3. The Corner Triangles The empty space inside the rectangle forms three distinct right-angled triangles ($T_1, T_2, T_3$). Their bases and heights are defined entirely by coordinate differences.
4. Area Subtraction The area of our main triangle is precisely the Area of the Rectangle minus the areas of $T_1$, $T_2$, and $T_3$.
5. Algebraic Expansion Substituting $\frac{1}{2}(\Delta x \cdot \Delta y)$ for each corner triangle and expanding all terms causes massive algebraic cancellation.
6. The Determinant Matrix The remaining terms factor perfectly into the classic $3 \times 3$ matrix determinant. Geometry perfectly matches linear algebra!

Algebraic Derivation

1. Area Subtraction

Let $\text{Rect} = (x_2 - x_1)(y_3 - y_1)$. The areas of the three right triangles are:

  • $T_1 = \frac{1}{2}(x_3 - x_1)(y_3 - y_1)$
  • $T_2 = \frac{1}{2}(x_2 - x_3)(y_3 - y_2)$
  • $T_3 = \frac{1}{2}(x_2 - x_1)(y_2 - y_1)$

The area of our target triangle is: $[ABC] = \text{Rect} - T_1 - T_2 - T_3$.

2. The Determinant Matrix

If you expand all binomials in the subtraction above, cancel the matching cross-terms, and factor out $\frac{1}{2}$, you are left with:

$$ [ABC] = \frac{1}{2} \Big| x_1(y_2 - y_3) + x_2(y_3 - y_1) + x_3(y_1 - y_2) \Big| $$

This expression is exactly the expansion by minors of the $3 \times 3$ determinant matrix containing the vertices' coordinates!
