The Geometric Proof

The Euclidean Algorithm finds the greatest common divisor (GCD) of two numbers $A$ and $B$ by repeatedly removing the largest square possible from a rectangle of size $A \times B$. What's left over after every square has been cut away tells us the GCD — and geometry shows us exactly why this works.

If the current rectangle has width $w$ and height $h$ with $w \ge h$, the biggest square that fits has side length $h$. We can cut exactly $q$ of these squares before the leftover strip is narrower than $h$.

The Divisor Never Changes

This geometric long division gives us the equation:

$$ w = q \cdot h + r, \qquad 0 \le r < h $$

Any number that perfectly divides both $w$ and $h$ must also perfectly divide the remainder $r = w - q \cdot h$. Therefore, the GCD stays exactly the same at every step:

$$ \gcd(w, h) = \gcd(r, h) $$

The cutting stops once a side is reduced to $0$ — at that moment, the leftover rectangle is a perfect square. Because every cut preserved the GCD, this final square's side length is the exact GCD!

$$ \gcd(A, B) = \text{side length of the final square} $$
