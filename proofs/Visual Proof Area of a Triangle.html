Visual Proof: $\text{Area of a Triangle} = \frac{1}{2} b h$

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} b h$$

How to understand this proof:

  1. Start with a Triangle: We begin with an arbitrary triangle of base length $b$ and height $h$.
  2. Rotate an Identical Copy ($180^\circ$): By rotating a duplicate copy of the triangle $180^\circ$ around the midpoint $M$ of side $BC$, the two triangles join to form a parallelogram with base $b$ and height $h$.
  3. Transform Parallelogram into a Rectangle: By cutting a triangular sliver from the left side along the perpendicular height line and sliding (translating) it to the right, it fits perfectly on the opposite end to build a plain $b \times h$ rectangle.
  4. The Final Conclusion: The area of the entire newly formed rectangle is simply $\text{width} \times \text{height} = b \times h$. Since this rectangle was constructed from exactly 2 identical copies of our original triangle, the area of one triangle must be exactly half:
$$\text{Area of Triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$$
