Proof Progression

1. The Point and the Line We want to find the perpendicular distance $d$ from the external point $P(x_0, y_0)$ to the line $Ax+By+C=0$.
2. Constructing the Base Pick an intercept point $Q$ on the line. Construct $R = Q + (B, -A)$. Because $(A,B) \cdot (B,-A) = 0$, $QR$ perfectly lies on the line. The length of this base is $\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$.
3. Transforming to a Rectangle Duplicate $\Delta PQR$ to form a parallelogram. Then horizontally shear it into a perfect rectangle. Because shearing preserves area, the combined area is exactly $d \sqrt{A^2+B^2}$.
4. Area via Determinant Using coordinates, the area of a parallelogram spanned by $\vec{QR}$ and $\vec{QP}$ is given by their absolute determinant: $|B(y_0-y_1) - (-A)(x_0-x_1)|$.
5. Equating the Areas The determinant simplifies beautifully to $|Ax_0+By_0+C|$. By equating this to our geometric rectangle area $d\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$, we derive the final distance formula!

Deriving the Distance Formula

1. The Geometric Area

By duplicating $\Delta PQR$, we form a parallelogram. As shown in the animation, a physical shear transforms this parallelogram into a rectangle without altering its area.

The base of this rectangle is the segment $QR$, which we constructed using the vector $(B, -A)$. Its length is exactly $\sqrt{B^2 + (-A)^2} = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2}$.

$$ \text{Area} = \text{base} \times \text{height} = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2} \cdot d $$

2. The Coordinate Area

Linear algebra tells us the area of a parallelogram spanned by two vectors is the absolute determinant of the matrix formed by those vectors. Let $\vec{QR} = (B, -A)$ and $\vec{QP} = (x_0-x_1, y_0-y_1)$.

$$ \text{Area} = \left| \det \begin{pmatrix} B & -A \\ x_0-x_1 & y_0-y_1 \end{pmatrix} \right| $$

Expanding this gives $|B(y_0 - y_1) + A(x_0 - x_1)|$. Rearranging terms, we get $|Ax_0 + By_0 - (Ax_1 + By_1)|$. Since $Q(x_1, y_1)$ is on the line $Ax+By+C=0$, we know $Ax_1 + By_1 = -C$. This perfectly simplifies the Area to $|Ax_0 + By_0 + C|$!
